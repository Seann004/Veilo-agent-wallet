Based on the text provided, here is an analysis of what makes community repair cafes succeed and what they measurably achieve.

### Key Claims and Evidence

**What makes community repair cafes succeed:**

1.  **Volunteer Retention and Support:** Success depends on keeping volunteers engaged.
    - **Evidence:** Volunteer interviews ([4]) show that teaching visitors is more rewarding than doing repairs for them, pairing new volunteers with experienced fixers is valued, and burnout risk rises when understaffed. Volunteers also requested short safety refreshers for electrical work.
2.  **Effective Scheduling and Outreach:** The timing of sessions and how they are promoted affects attendance.
    - **Evidence:** A pilot of weekday evening sessions ([8]) attracted a younger, first-time visitor demographic, though fewer visitors overall than Saturdays. Event listings published less than three days ahead had lower turnout, and personal repair stories in newsletters drew more reader replies than simple event listings ([5]).
3.  **Availability of Spare Parts:** The ability to complete repairs is heavily dependent on sourcing parts.
    - **Evidence:** A parts sourcing diary ([6]) found that proprietary parts often require waiting several weeks, and some manufacturers' parts cannot be purchased by individuals. A shared parts shelf reduced repeat trips for common items. Local business partnerships ([11]) can help, with shops willing to donate end-of-line stock.
4.  **Clear Information and Logistics:** Addressing visitor needs and logistical hurdles improves the experience.
    - **Evidence:** An exit survey ([2]) found visitors asked for clearer parts information. Business partnership notes ([11]) mention that hosting sessions requires public liability arrangements, and shops valued being mentioned in event promotion.

**What they measurably achieve:**

1.  **Items Repaired and Waste Diverted:** The cafes successfully repair a significant portion of items brought in, diverting them from landfill.
    - **Evidence:** The Harbourview repair cafe attendance log ([1]) recorded 418 items brought in, with 61% marked as fully repaired on the day. A method note ([7]) describes how the group estimates the weight of items kept out of landfill, reporting it as a range. Bicycles and furniture dominate the weight estimate even though they are a small share of items.
2.  **Visitor Motivations and Demographics:** The cafes attract visitors primarily motivated by saving money and reducing waste, and they can reach new demographics.
    - **Evidence:** An exit survey ([2]) found 54% of visitors named saving money as their main reason, and 33% named reducing waste. The weekday evening pilot ([8]) attracted a younger group, many of whom were first-time visitors.
3.  **Educational Impact:** The cafes provide hands-on learning opportunities.
    - **Evidence:** School workshop feedback ([10]) shows students reported increased confidence using basic tools, and most had never opened an electronic device before.
4.  **Tool Library Usage:** A related tool library model shows high usage and the value of specialist tools.
    - **Evidence:** The Eastside tool library ([3]) lent 1,940 items to 312 members in its first year. Specialist tools were borrowed rarely but valued highly by members who used them.
